# Introduction & Purpose

This document restates, in organized prose, everything currently written in the vault's **Umm**, **Pan**, **Pot**, and **Shoe** notes — covering cosmology, history, deities, peoples, geography, cities, organizations, and flora & fauna. Nothing here is invented; every claim is drawn from an existing note. Where the notes are thin, silent, or contradict one another, a footnote flags it rather than papering over the gap.[^purpose]

The setting's own one-line pitch, taken directly from the vault: *"The Kingdom of Pot and the greater continent of Pan is a campaign setting designed around whimsy. Its inspiration is primarily taken from Adventure Time and Studio Ghibli. This manifests in themes of regrowth and reconstruction. The major cities of Pan are built upon the ruins of prehistoric civilizations, and the magic of the world is weak and untapped."*

Stated inspirations (from the "Gygax 75" planning notes) include *Princess Mononoke*, *Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind*, *Adventure Time*, *Dying Earth*, *Fantasy High*, and *Undertale*. The core pitch: a refurbished, recycled world built atop older civilizations, teeming with strange life, where magic runs through everything but is only skillfully wielded by the trained or spiritual, where powerful beasts become guardians of their domain, and where player characters are among the rare few with real curiosity and wanderlust.

[^purpose]: Per your request, every discrepancy, contradiction, unfinished section, or notable coverage gap found while compiling this document is called out in a numbered footnote like this one, rather than silently smoothed over or invented around.

# Part I — Cosmology & History

## The Eras of Ümm

The vault's history file lays out a mytho-historical timeline in eight eras. It is restated here in full, since it is the backbone everything else hangs on.

**1. Era of Others.** The All-Makers create other continents and worlds, separated by the Astral Sea. Many of these worlds fail and become the Wasted Barrens that separate the continents of life from one another. Ümm itself is named as one of these — "a world similar to modern life on Earth, but destroyed and ruined by the explosion of magic that warped the world into great chaos before settling into waste." The All-Makers create by collectively dreaming; the further a continent drifts from the most recent dream, the "fuzzier" it becomes.

**2. Era of Emptiness.** The All-Makers create the continent of Pan, then mungrass and Claymen. Dissatisfied with its own creativity, it creates an "egg of randomagus," which births Zerula.

**3. Era of the Titans.** Zerula, Queen of the Titans, begins creating life beyond the Claymen and mungrass. She creates the Titans — forces of nature embodied as deities — and her sister Za'esitor (Princess of the Titans) and Cicebeo (Prince of the Gods). Za'esitor is sent to explore the edges of the world while Cicebeo maintains the known lands. Zerula dissipates her own magic into the land, creating the first inherent magic and the first guardians. Cicebeo creates humans, then other gods. Za'esitor returns with knowledge of powerful channeled magic and is appalled by what Cicebeo has built. She sends eight disciples — one per school of magic — to curse eight great Cicebian cities (sparing the inhabitants but sealing the cities off from the world), saving a ninth curse for herself. Named among the eight: the **Plastic City** (illusion), the **Forest of Giant Things** (transmutation), the city of **Mountmouth** (enchantment), and the **Abyssal City** (conjuration). The other five Cicebian cities are unnamed.[^cicebian-cities] Enraged, Cicebeo and the other gods seal Za'esitor into the Astral Sea, but Cicebeo can never reverse the curses. The gods eventually grow bored of the Cicebians' slow rebuilding and go dormant.

[^cicebian-cities]: **Coverage gap.** Only 4 of the 9 cursed Cicebian cities are named anywhere in the vault (Plastic City/illusion, Forest of Giant Things/transmutation, Mountmouth/enchantment, Abyssal City/conjuration). The other five schools of magic (evocation, necromancy, abjuration, divination, and Za'esitor's own personal ninth curse) have no associated city anywhere in the notes.

**4. Era of Formation (~8,000–20,000 years ago).** With gods and titans dormant, the scattered Cicebian peoples rebuild on small scales and rebind into new nations.

**5. Era of Darkness (~3,000–8,000 years ago).** War and slavery reign. Inland humans are treated as the "mutt" of all races, enslaved by the Drow. Orcs and goblinoids are used as war-troops for their lack of national identity — except in Elvilae, which refuses to use orcs for warfare.

**6. Era of Reformation (~2,000–3,000 years ago).** Merus invents mechanical (codified) magic and travels the human lands of Shaterlan, empowering them. This raises human standing with other nations and fuels rebellion against the Drow. With help from Goldpan and the Kingdom of Pot, humans found the nation-states of Freland, Merusia, Franklin, Delvania, and Merukirk. Orcs, no longer propelled by war and slavery, scatter to the realm's corners.

**7. The Atutarunian Era (~1,000–2,000 years ago).** Peace holds for a time as human nations gain standing and Shaterlan's power wanes. Seeing its influence slipping, Shaterlan goes to war with the Kingdom of Pot, Merusia, Freland, Delvania, and Trawlittle.[^trawlittle-spelling] From the western isles, a god-picked warlord named **Atalan Autarun**[^atalan-stub] rises, uniting the isles and conquering northeastward across Pan — bow to him or be annihilated, though he incorporates conquered nations' own generals into his army and, by the notes' own account, "sleeps around" as he goes, gaining a fair bit of respect along the way. He skips conquering Pot directly (citing its defensible geography) in favor of destroying the Drow in the northeast — until his half-elf general **Opelanon** betrays him and strikes Pot anyway, against orders. Atalan turns his army around, destroys Opelanon's forces, and "disappears" Opelanon. He then destroys the Drow, forcing them back underground into the Underdark, and forms a continental government/parliament, heading it for a decade before passing the role to a descendant.

[^trawlittle-spelling]: **Minor discrepancy.** This region's name is spelled two different ways across the vault: "Trawlittle" (used in *The History of Umm*, *Peoples of Umm*, and the Countries Index) and "Tralittle" (used in *Gnomes of Umm*, where the Tea Gnomes are said to live "in Tralittle in both the North and South"). It's unclear which spelling is canonical.

[^atalan-stub]: **Coverage gap.** Atalan Autarun single-handedly drives the entire seventh era of the setting's history and is namechecked repeatedly elsewhere (the "Herd of the Horse Lord" faith worships him and hopes to inherit his bloodline; Isle of Man humans get a whole subrace built around his cavalry legacy; the Felling Festival in Shoe partly celebrates the "unifier Atalan Autarun"). Despite this, his dedicated character page (`Atalan Autarun.md`) contains nothing but a title and two aliases ("Yu Autarun," "Horse Lord") — no stats, description, or additional history.

**8. The Modern Era.** Described in the notes as "a time of relative peace," followed by "a big bad guy does a thing and is shut down hard when it goes beyond," another unspecified time, "the Merusian Conquest," and "another time of relative peace."[^modern-era-stub] The current in-setting year is referenced once, in the Homepage, as "the clean year of ___" — the number was never filled in.[^blank-year]

[^modern-era-stub]: **Major coverage gap.** The entire Modern Era — presumably the era the campaign is actually set in — is written as unfinished placeholder bullets rather than actual history: "A big bad guy does a thing and is shut down hard when it goes beyond," "A time," "Another time of relative peace." The "Merusian Conquest" is named but never described anywhere else in the vault.

[^blank-year]: **Gap.** The Homepage's line "Right now, in the clean year of" is left incomplete — no current year is recorded anywhere in the notes.

## Guardians

A short but important standalone definition file exists for **Guardians**: *"beings blessed by the Titans with supernatural power. They represent the true nature of a place or location and maintain that nature. Titans do not choose particular beings to be their guardians — instead, the beast's connection to an essence creates the bond. The longer a location has stayed the same, the more powerful the Guardian."* This same definition is duplicated nearly verbatim inside the Deities file under "Minor Deities."[^duplicate-guardian] Guardians are described elsewhere (Quick Ideas) as the entities modern people actually revere day-to-day, with the Titans and Gods themselves mostly forgotten.

[^duplicate-guardian]: **Redundancy.** The definition of a Guardian is written out in full twice — once in its own dedicated file (`Guardian.md`) and again, almost word-for-word, inside `Deities.md`. Not contradictory, just duplicated.

### Tuseed, God of Propagation — full writeup

The one god given a complete dedicated page. Title: God of Propagation. Domain: Nature, Trickery. Alignment: Chaotic Neutral. Indicator of Patronage: kudzu clippings, live or dead rats, rabbit ears/feet, and/or mushrooms in a small pouch. Symbol: a vine around a tree. Ideal: *"The greater the quantity, the greater the quality. A thousand swords is always greater than one well-made sword."* Avatar Appearance: a swarm of rats, a bunch of bunnies, or a whipping wind full of air-borne seeds. Typical Worshipers: farmers. Boons scale from a 1gp tribute (restoring a known plant to health) up to a 100gp tribute (touch any known plant and have a 30ft radius around it fill with mature specimens within a minute).

## Guardians & Champions (Minor Deities)

Minor deities are almost always either **Guardians** (see Part I) or **Champions** — "the chosen of the gods, blessed with their own divine power. They represent the god's will for change... gods can have any number of champions, but often no more than three, so as not to dilute their power. The greater the change a champion creates in the shorter amount of time, the more powerful they grow." One named Guardian, **Parcel**, appears in the deity table with alignment Neutral, domain Tempest, and symbol "a circling whale" — but is elsewhere (Umm Homepage) described as "Parcel, the Sky Whale," co-worshipped alongside the Titan Seamus by the House of Hurricanes, which is a considerably higher-profile role than the table's brief "Guardian" listing would suggest.[^parcel-status]

[^parcel-status]: **Possible discrepancy.** Guardians are defined elsewhere as *place-bound* entities that "represent the true nature of a place or location" — yet Parcel, tagged as a Guardian in the deity table, is worshipped continent-wide (or at least region-wide) as a named co-deity of an entire faith (the House/Church of Hurricanes), which sits oddly with the Guardian definition's emphasis on being bound to a single location.

## Wan-Jer

A separate philosophy/tradition, not classed under the Old Hope: the **Wan-Jer**, *"focused on nature, resourcefulness, independence, and tradition. True Wan-Jer are nomadic druids who tend the land's flora, fauna, and natural resources; others who can't commit to that lifestyle practice the traditions and rituals instead."* Spread across Pan but concentrated in the West. A named sub-tradition, **Second Sister Masrasa**, blends Wan-Jer interconnectedness with an Old Hope-style following of "the Mother of Morcs, the second sister" — tying the Wan-Jer obliquely into the Orc-origin myth (see Part III).

## Mission of Merus

The newest of the three "core faiths" (the notes don't explicitly name the other two, but context suggests the Old Hope and the Wan-Jer). *"[Mission of Merus] focuses on outreach, education, and innovation through magic. Practiced mainly in the east, where its founder Merus created the first spellbook and shared his cantrips throughout the lands."*[^merus-corefaiths] Its denominations:

- **Protoatong** — the primary denomination; believes in collective education, charity, outreach, and progress through innovation. Temples resemble libraries more than churches.
- **Gnomer** — focused on personal knowledge and enlightenment; followers write their own books of truths and spells (modeled on Merus's own) and reject all outside knowledge until they've personally confirmed it.
- **Nailian Orthodox** — an eastern (Nailia) denomination holding that Merus began as a Nailian before heading east, and that before he vanished he returned with a second, more advanced spellbook, sealed in a tomb hidden in the eastern dunes.

[^merus-corefaiths]: **Unstated assumption.** The Homepage calls the Mission of Merus "the newest of the three core faiths" but never names what the other two "core faiths" are. Based on the surrounding structure of the page, they are most likely the Old Hope and the Wan-Jer, but this is inferred, not stated.

# Part III — Peoples of Ümm

The vault frames the world's population as split into two broad camps (per the Quick Ideas file): **Monsters** — wild-living races with simple wants, small communities, who keep to themselves — and **Non-monsters** — the humanoid races who go out into the world to conquer, learn, and understand its power. A second, overlapping framing splits people into **change-bringers** (active, ambitious) and **calm-keepers** (passive, maintaining the status quo).

## Standard Ancestries

**Dragonborn.** Stronghold in the South, in Nailia and Goldpan. Most live in the desert city of **Blue**, inheriting its color, or the swamp city of **Muk** among the tar dwarves (green-and-black Dragonborn). Every color appears scattered across Ümm, with unusually silver ones found in the scavenging city of Shoe.[^blue-muk-nolink]

**Dwarves.** Three primary homes: the winding mountains of **Stonton** (wind and steel, "the whistle of the wind and the song of the steel"), the north of **Trawlittle** as seafarers and ice-tamers, and the far South as frontiersmen and mud-divers. A separate, thin note on "Cloud Song Dwarves" describes a Song Steel-forging, backpacking culture that lives in temporary mountain shacks around one great communal forge — unclear if this is the same group as the Stonton dwarves or a fourth, undescribed subgroup.[^dwarves-thin]

**Elves.** Arrived on Pan in three waves. The first landed in Elvilae, intertwining deeply with Goblinoid escapist history; marked by blond-to-silver hair and blue-toned skin. The second wave ("the Drowned") were lost at sea in a great astral storm and mysteriously turned up in the tunnels beneath Shaterlan — becoming, implicitly, the Drow. The third wave came to claim dominion over all of Ümm but were stopped and isolated when the gate to the Astral Sea closed; those isolated at sea, or who chose to stay there, were warped by its magic into Astral Elves.[^elves-nolink]

**Gnomes.** Come in three sizes — small, medium, and large — though nobody has seen a large gnome in eons, and the small ones are mostly folklore thanks to their stealth. Two named subspecies:
  - *Tea Gnomes* (small) — live in the South of Trawlittle among similarly small wildlife, and in the North of Trawlittle in what's described as "a spy state amongst the Ice Dwarves," as well as the thicket forests of Merukirk. Expert at shrinking and camouflaging themselves as pinecones, rocks, or branches, using large hats to complete the disguise. Culturally secretive and exclusionary; they liken themselves more to animals and seeds than to normal humanoids.
  - *May Gnomes* (medium) — closer to elves in appearance, with perfectly horizontal ears and triangular noses; rumored to be the "ugly duckling" children of Tea Gnomes who never learned to shrink. Outcasts and rebels, often ending up as criminals in gangs or sulking intellectuals. Found mainly in Franklin, Merukirk, and Delvania.
  - The medium-sized mainstream Gnomes (mentioned in *Peoples of Umm* but not detailed under *Gnomes of Umm*) are said to live in the East of Stonton where the mountains meet Deepwater Lake, carving roads and isolated sanctuary-cities into the rock.[^gnome-medium-gap]

**Halflings.** The entry in *Peoples of Umm* simply reads: *"Unfinished..."*[^halflings-tieflings-gap]

**Humans.** Live primarily on the Eastern Coast of Pot, across Merusia, Freland, Franklin, and Merukirk, with scattered populations in Shaterlan and the Southwest (Isle of Man). Nationality determines minor cosmetic and mechanical variation:
  - *Shaterfolk* (Shaterlan) — centuries of subjugation and enslavement in caves and shadow bred adaptations for darkness: larger eyes/ears, paler skin, faded lime-green/pink/lavender eye colors sometimes gleaming faintly on the skin. Post-liberation, they diversified into every skillset to fill society's cracks.
  - *Merusian* (Merusia) — slightly pointed ears, brown-orange eyes; the first people (of any species) taught codified/mechanical magic by Merus himself, a legacy of pride that has fed recent conflict.
  - *Franklin & Merukirk* — simple, quiet, well-rounded lives.
  - *Isle of Man / Autarunian* — gold eyes, deep crimson hair; homeland of Atalan Autarun; proud martial/cavalry culture.
  - *Otherlander* — humans fully integrated into a non-human-majority nation.

**Orcs.** Split into three primary clans — **Worcs** (strong, sturdy, gray-skinned, blocky features, combat-focused), **Gorcs** (intelligent, devoted, blue-skinned, classic Orc features, academically focused), and **Morcs** (spiritual, empathetic, orange-skinned, elephant-like features) — plus unnamed smaller clans. Origin myth (found only inside the *Orcs of Pot* character-creation file, not in any Lore file): Cicebeo's creation of humans upset the Titan Aug, who made the *original* orcs as a force of nature meant to check human expansion. After centuries of orc-driven ruin, Cicebeo hunted down and slew the Orc King, Korc, splitting his body into three daughters — Gora (mother of the Gorcs, "the mind"), Waera (mother of the Worcs, "the body"), and Moira (mother of the Morcs, "the spirit"). Legend holds a fourth, undivided part of Korc remained "wholly devoted to ruin," birthing the feral, ungodly orcs from which (implicitly) modern monstrous orc-kin descend.
  - *Gorcs* get a full mechanical writeup, with two named subspecies: **High Gorcs** (fully assimilated academics/leaders, elaborate braided hair, embellished academic robes) and **Romantic Gorcs** (primal, nomadic knowledge-hoarders who travel in "schools" and adorn themselves with the spoils of their learning, sometimes by force).
  - *Half-Gorcs* (Gorc/Human) get a full writeup too — sky-blue-skinned, kinder and less knowledge-hungry than full Gorcs, common across Pot but concentrated in cities.
  - *Worcs* and *Morcs* are named repeatedly but have **no dedicated writeup anywhere in the vault**.[^worcs-morcs-gap]

**Tieflings.** Like Halflings, the entry simply reads: *"Unfinished..."*[^halflings-tieflings-gap]

**Goblins.** *Peoples of Umm* only links out to a dedicated Goblins file, which itself is thin: Goblinoids landed in Delvania and Elvilae; those in Delvania were pulled into "the conflict of the 7 great vampire lords" (a conflict never described anywhere else in the vault[^vampire-lords-gap]); a separate note mentions **Astral Goblins**, said to resemble the MTG creature Gimbal/gremlin.

[^blue-muk-nolink]: **Coverage gap.** The Dragonborn cities of "Blue" and "Muk" are named but have no dedicated location pages anywhere in the vault.

[^elves-nolink]: **Coverage gap / broken reference.** The Peoples of Umm file links "Elves" to a page called "Elves of Pot," but no such file exists anywhere in the vault — this is the only ancestry description in that file whose linked detail page is entirely missing (compare to Dwarves, Gnomes, and Goblins, which do have dedicated pages, if thin ones).

[^dwarves-thin]: **Coverage gap.** The dedicated Dwarves file (`Dwarves of Pot.md`) contains only a three-bullet "Quick Notes" fragment about "Cloud Song Dwarves" and never actually describes the Stonton, Trawlittle, or Southern dwarf cultures named in *Peoples of Umm* — it's unclear if Cloud Song Dwarves are a fourth group or one of the three already named.

[^gnome-medium-gap]: **Coverage gap.** The mainstream medium-sized Gnomes (Stonton/Deepwater Lake dwellers) are described briefly in *Peoples of Umm* but have no corresponding subspecies entry in the dedicated *Gnomes of Umm* file, which only details Tea Gnomes and May Gnomes.

[^halflings-tieflings-gap]: **Explicit, author-acknowledged gap.** Both the Halfling and Tiefling entries in *Peoples of Umm* consist of the single word "Unfinished..."

[^worcs-morcs-gap]: **Coverage gap.** Worcs and Morcs are named as two of the three primary Orc clans and given physical descriptions in the origin myth, but unlike Gorcs and Half-Gorcs, neither has a dedicated species/mechanics writeup anywhere in the vault. The base `Orcs of Pot.md` file itself is also, structurally, an unfilled template — its "Appearance," "Culture," "Status," "Adventuring," and "Species Traits" sections are all still generic placeholder instruction text (e.g., *"A description of the species culture including usual social structure..."*), with only the origin-myth paragraph actually written.

[^vampire-lords-gap]: **Coverage gap.** "The conflict of the 7 great vampire lords" is referenced as the event that pulled Delvania's goblinoids into history, but no file anywhere describes who these seven vampire lords were or what the conflict entailed.

## Homebrew / Exotic Peoples

These are original species, mostly documented in the Character Creation → Species folder rather than the Lore → Peoples folder — an organizational split that puts some "peoples" content in a different part of the vault than others.[^species-split]

**Weirds.** Non-sentient elemental beings (fire/water/earth/air, and their combinations — steam, magma, smoke, mud, ice, dust) bound to a location, until a magical artifact ("anchor") falls into or is placed in their elemental source (a volcano, pool, dune, etc.), granting sentience and mobility. Split into **Flukes** (accidental, wandering, appearance in flux) and **Formed** (intentionally created by a clan for a purpose, with a fixed youth-appearance and a later self-chosen mature appearance granted at their coming-of-age "Rite of Many Faces"). Formed Weirds' lifespans (80–200 years) are tied to their anchor's finite magical charge; after death, an anchor can be recovered and recharged to create a new Weird, forming a pseudo-lineage. Weirds have no biological sex and don't reproduce. The Bibb chapel-tending Water Weirds (see Part II, House of Hurricanes) are the setting's one confirmed example of a Formed Weird clan with a specific religious purpose.

**Wildlings.** Humanoid-animal hybrids, all descended from **Veranthropes** — lycanthropes who "ascended beyond the separation of beast and humanoid, reconciling the two." Modern Wildlings mostly descend from the original enlightened few and live in small, species-segregated hamlets across Pot, sometimes retaining fragments of their ancestral cultures (Mouslings live like Halflings; Lionins live like nomadic southern human tribes). A full customization framework is provided for building new Wildling "flavors" out of a weighted trait-point budget.

**Enoki.** Sentient mushroom people, fruiting bodies separated from a collective mycelial "dream"/unconscious network. Lack necks; body and limbs are extensions of their stalk. Young Enoki are raised by colony elders, leave home to adventure and gather experience, and eventually return home — ultimately rejoining the network itself and passing their life's experience back into the collective dream. Cannot speak verbally (they hum, whistle, grumble) but communicate via touch-based telepathy and "rapport spores." Names are self-chosen phrases drawn from the dream upon leaving the colony (e.g., "Leaves Falling in Moonlight," "Ships Burning at Sea"). Rare in Shoe (~5% of population) but common in the shaded groves and forests around Pot.

**Astralians.** Silent, mouthless visitors from the Astral Plane, present since long before written history. Clammy lime-green to dark pale-gray skin, small ear-holes, large black oval eyes. Usually found in pods of two to four, favoring high altitudes and hot or cold deserts. Communicate via telepathy; possess minor innate psionic/illusion magic (Dancing Lights, later Dissonant Whispers and Abduction).

**Goozers.** Reformed sentient oozes from abandoned ancient cities and dungeons, who over centuries in solitude developed consciousness and sought out humanoid company or shape. Three subspecies: **Grays** (small, corrode metal, camouflage well in gloomy environments), **Translucents** (largest, can adopt a cube-like "engulfing" form), and **Ochres** (mid-sized, can split off small jelly-spawn when struck by lightning or slashing damage, climb walls). Very rare (~0.5% of Pot's population); tend to cluster in the decrepit, abandoned parts of Fofam, Shoe, and Mountmouth, forming small enclaves accessible only to other Goozers. Names are typically adopted after integrating into humanoid society — mumbled baby-babble names, appearance-based names ("Slimebo," "Gooey"), or fully human names given by adoptive communities.

**Automatons.** Mechanical beings of three forms: **True Automatons** (ancient, origin lost to time, self-sufficient power source, rest in an inert "sentry" state rather than sleeping), **Scrap Chaps** (brave, field-repairable by anyone with artisan tools), and **Fishborgs** (all built by a single named inventor, Jeremy Fishborg, a fisherman once saved by fish who now builds mechanical vessels to let especially intelligent fish live on land; a Fishborg's "true self" is a small fish that can retreat into a bowl built into the automaton chassis if the body is destroyed).

**Gignomes.** A short, single-paragraph entry: "Gigantic gnomes living in the Forest of Giant Things. They look like lawn gnomes but 10–12ft tall and squashed down."[^gignomes-thin]

[^species-split]: **Organizational inconsistency, not a lore contradiction.** Dwarves, Gnomes, and Goblins are filed under `Lore/Peoples`, while Humans, Orcs, Weirds, Wildlings, Enoki, Astralians, Goozers, and Automatons are filed under `Character Creation/Species`. Both folders describe "peoples" of the setting; the split appears to be organizational rather than intentional (i.e., there's no apparent lore reason some ancestries are "Lore" and others are "Character Creation").

[^gignomes-thin]: **Coverage gap.** Gignomes get a single descriptive sentence and no culture, history, or relationship to the Forest of Giant Things' curse (see Part IV) or to regular Gnomes.

# Part V — The Kingdom of Pot

## Overview

### History

Two named conflicts define recent Pottian history:

- **Opelanon's Invasion** (~150 years ago) — the human firebrand general who broke from Atalan Autarun to attack Pot against orders (see Part I). Described as recent enough that "the remnants of the war" still linger, with a small standing army split among Pot's three cities.[^which-three-cities]
- **Deghibabt's Invasion** (~1,000 years ago) — a firebrand lich who tried to conquer the land and was believed exterminated; quiet rumors persist that he is "regaining power in the far reaches."

[^which-three-cities]: **Ambiguity.** "A small standing army is split among the three cities" is stated without specifying which three of Pot's several named cities (Shoe, Fofam, Mountmouth, Bibb, Flip, Flop, Slipper, Norget) those are.

### Economy

One economic resource is named: **animalapilanimite**, "the precious gem that is the animating force for automatons" — presumably the power source referenced in the Automatons species entry, though the two files never cross-reference each other explicitly.[^animalapilanimite-link]

[^animalapilanimite-link]: **Unlinked but likely connection.** The Automatons species file never names its power source, and the Kingdom of Pot economy file never explains how animalapilanimite is mined, traded, or who controls it — the connection between the two is a reasonable inference, not a stated fact.

### Seasons & Calendar

Pot runs on a four-season calendar: **Drenched, Dreg, Dry,** and **Drag.** Per the Kingdom of Pot file: Drenched lasts 12 weeks of 12 days (144 days); Drag lasts 4 weeks of 12 days plus one 4-day week (52 days); Dry lasts 12 weeks of 12 days (144 days); and Dreg lasts 3 weeks of 12 days plus one single-day week, called the **Grand Finally** (37 days). That totals 377 days in a year.[^calendar-math]

The Shoe city file describes the same four seasons, but states instead that *"years are 251–254 days long, with the dry and drenched seasons being about 100–120 days long and the drag and dreg seasons being 5–20 days long,"* and names the capstone event the **Grand Vinali** rather than the Grand Finally — with a full 1d12+1d4 weather table attached to it. The `Shoe (Secret).md` file uses "Grand Finally" again (matching the Kingdom of Pot file) and folds Dreg and Drag into one combined weather table rather than treating them separately, as `Shoe.md` does.[^season-length-discrepancy]

[^calendar-math]: **Internal math note.** 144 + 52 + 144 + 37 = 377 days total, using the Kingdom of Pot file's own week/day breakdown.

[^season-length-discrepancy]: **Numeric and naming discrepancy across three files.** (1) `Kingdom of Pot.md` implies a ~377-day year via its week-by-week breakdown. (2) `Shoe.md` explicitly states years are "251–254 days long." (3) The capstone weather event is called the **Grand Finally** in both `Kingdom of Pot.md` and `Shoe (Secret).md`, but the **Grand Vinali** in the public `Shoe.md` — with `Shoe.md` alone providing the full weather roll table for it. It is not clear which day-count or which name is meant to be canonical, or whether "Grand Finally" and "Grand Vinali" are meant to be two names for the same thing or a leftover renaming that wasn't fully propagated.

Also the launch point for the Skydivers Guild's descents into the Abyssal City below.
- **Mountmouth** — a major city on the northeastern side of the basin, built atop an ancient, abandoned dwarven mine. One of only three "large cities" named anywhere in the setting (alongside Shoe and Fofam, per the Quick Ideas file). Beyond this one sentence, nothing else is recorded about it, despite Mountmouth being one of the nine legendary Cicebian Cities (cursed with enchantment magic — see Part I).[^mountmouth-thin]
- **Bibb** — a major city of mostly wood elves at the very northeastern tip of Pot, guarding the entrance to the Pot basin without technically being part of the kingdom; the two maintain a wary non-interference pact, though Bibb has repeatedly helped defend Pot's alternate entrances during past invasions. Built atop an older, more ornate high-elf settlement that current wood-elf residents deliberately let stay overgrown. Home to the confirmed physical seat of House of Hurricanes worship (see Part II).

- **Flop** — named only as Flip's sister city; no dedicated page or description exists.[^flop-missing]
- **Slipper** — a single sentence: "the little sister city of the great city of Shoe."
- **Waterbridge** — named only in the Kingdom of Pot city index; no dedicated page exists.[^waterbridge-missing]
- **Norget** — has a dedicated page, but the page is completely blank.[^norget-blank]

[^fofam-capital]: **Unconfirmed elsewhere.** Fofam's own page calls it "the capital city of the Kingdom of Pot," but no other file (including the Kingdom of Pot's own overview, or the Umm Homepage) independently confirms this or discusses a royal seat, court, or ruler there. Given how much more narrative attention Shoe receives elsewhere, it's worth double-checking at the table whether Fofam or Shoe is meant to be the "main" city.

[^flop-missing]: **Coverage gap.** Flop is referenced by name (as Flip's sister city) but has no dedicated page.

Two more locations round out Pot's geography with essentially no detail: **Varawae**, which has a dedicated but entirely blank page,[^varawae-blank] and the region around the two rivers, **Westwit River** and **Surrit River**, which are named but not described anywhere.

# Part VI — The City of Shoe

Shoe is, by far, the most thoroughly documented location in the entire vault, and functions as the setting's default "home base."

## Entering the City

Approaching from the southern entrance, travelers pass outer wheat fields before reaching Shoe's outer cobble wall, guarded loosely. Past the gate lies the southernmost outer ward — a cheerful, business-but-not-stressful middle-class neighborhood of detached and connected houses along wide, tree-lined streets. The road winds up cracked tan-marble steps to the grand, 50-foot marble inner wall, overgrown with lime-green and dark-olive vines and pink blossoms, guarded by two massive, centuries-still shield guardian statues. Past that gate lies **the Stretch**, Shoe's central hub, encompassing the Main Stretch, the Park of Shoe, and Platton.

## Districts & Neighborhoods

- **The Main Stretch.** The commercial and cultural heart of the city, built into and around the bones of the original citadel — wide marble towers and rectangular white houses now crowded by newer buildings "like vines constricting a dying tree," strung with rope bridges and squeezed into every gap. Full of vendors, tourists, pickpockets, and wildlife (nesting chicks, rooftop cats, thieving rats, wandering dogs). Notable establishments (detailed further below): Gnim's Noodles/Nickle's Copper Coffee, Darby's Identifying Stand, Frosty's Familiars, Nicks and Nack's, Shrimp's Tacos, the Wicked Wraith tavern, Brisk's Blessings, the Hanging Hostel, and Herb's herbalist shop.
- **The Park of Shoe.** West of the Stretch, still within the middle walls — described as the city's "slow, constant beating heart" versus the Main Stretch's "coronal artery." Former citadel parkland, now home to the open market, the city's churches, its guild halls, and a scattering of odd manors: the Scavengers Guild ("stingy sons of bitches," per the notes, who control Plastic City access), the Slayers Guild, the Church of New Covenant, the Temple of the Caretakers of Zerula, the Seamanite Assembly, the Smith's Guild, the Governance Brewery, the Mayor's Manor, the Odd Manor, the Bank of Shoe, the Wiltsman-Anderburg-Rondag Group (a private equity firm for Shoe's wealthiest), and the Open Air Market.
- **Platton.** The innermost ward, shadowing the Main Stretch behind 75-foot ancient citadel walls with corner barbicans. Vine- and fruit-tree-covered, retaining much of the original citadel structure alongside newer Victorian-style manors connected by stone paths and wrought-iron fences, centered on a large twisting cherry tree. Home to the Library of Shoe and to noble manors such as Hildenmatser Manor and Kane/Mutt Manor (see below).
- **The Shady Side.** Built against the western cliffside outside the "official" city, in a permanent low cloud (a cloudy day elsewhere is a rainy day here; rain elsewhere is a snowstorm here). Technically part of Shoe but not treated as such by local government. Home to the city's vagrants, open-daylight criminals, and paupers, in densely packed, shoddily built (and often boarded-up) apartment blocks, with sewage piling up in the alleys — but also, per the notes, "a certain hope to this place": street dice and board games, potted ferns and window plants, and neighbors feeding birds and the homeless when they can.
- **The Industrial District.** Everything that smokes, clangs, or stinks — automaton reclamation centers, armorers, weaponsmiths, brick-makers, warehouses. Thick low fog most mornings, flickering light most nights. Relatively crime-free thanks to constant patrol by safety inspectors and tax collectors rather than city guard.
- **High Tower.** The smallest neighborhood, in the southwest, built around the largest barbican of the middle wall. High elves live in the barbican itself; the surrounding lower neighborhood is a quiet, orderly middle-class mix of halflings, half-elves, gnomes, and a few lawful wildlings — gnome houses built as tall narrow towers, halfling houses short and wide with elaborate gardens.
- **South Field.** The largest neighborhood, primarily Gorcs, Half-Gorcs, Wildlings, Humans, and Dwarves, with smaller populations of fairies, gnomes, elves, and halflings. Less cramped than the Main Stretch, with sturdier wattle-and-daub construction, food gardens instead of lawns, and market stalls instead of benches; smells of candles, fire, and fruit.
- **East Gate.** A middle-sized, primarily orc/half-orc/goliath/dragonborn/weird/tiefling/enoki/goblin neighborhood — not poor, but rougher around the edges than South Field, with vine-swallowed abandoned homes standing beside a few large ornate houses. Street-food carts and busking bards are common.
- **Starboard.** A small, lavish riverside neighborhood of colonial houses facing well-kept docks, cabanas, and boat houses; home to museums, art houses, upscale boutiques, taverns, and coffeehouses. Blends messily into its neighbors at the edges — abandoned mills toward the Industrial District, mold toward the Shady Side.

## Culture

**Downtime activities** include local/regional theatre and **Brobble** — the region's signature sport, described in near-identical detail in two separate files.[^brobble-duplicate] It resembles football crossed with safe gladiatorial combat: teams alternate four "drives," choosing a ball loadout each drive (3 tossers worth 1 point each, 2 passers worth 2 points, or 1 runner worth 5 points); a ball that touches the ground is live for the other team to claim, and a drive is overturned if the defense claims every ball in play.

[^brobble-duplicate]: **Redundancy.** The full rules of Brobble are written out nearly verbatim in both `Brobble.md` (its own dedicated Organizations-folder file) and inside `Shoe (Secret).md`. Not contradictory, just duplicated word-for-word in two places.

**Festivals, events, and holidays:** the Felling Festival (celebrating the fall of Opelanon and honoring Atalan Autarun); Barrelsmash Day (smashing old barrels to prevent a "barrel goblin" infestation); the week-long Grand Vinali Festival leading up to the Grand Vinali weather event itself; Founder's Day (honoring the city's founder, Zapadoa Shoe[^founder-name]); and the Harvest Festival, which has no further description.

[^founder-name]: **Naming note.** The founder is named "Zapadoa Shoe" in the Main Stretch's Founder's Day entry, but the separate `Shoe (Secret).md` timeline simply calls the founding figure "Shoe" without the first name Zapadoa — not necessarily contradictory (a first name being added is a small elaboration, not a conflict), but worth flagging since the two aren't cross-referenced.

**Weather & the calendar** are covered above in Part V, including the season-length and Grand Vinali/Grand Finally naming discrepancy.

## The Secret History of Shoe

A separate, unpublished (`dg-publish: false`) file, `Shoe (Secret).md`, lays out a timeline that never appears in the public-facing `Shoe.md` file at all:[^shoe-secret-unlinked]

**Ancient (~1,000 years ago):** Founding by a figure named Shoe → Opelanon's invasion sparks the city's growth → Shoe's incorporation into Pot → a "planar shift" sparks a further wave of population growth → Deghibabt's invasion sparks more growth → the founder Shoe dies and is replaced, by the appointment of a figure named "Fee," with a new leader named **Enor**.

**Modern Era (~300 years ago):** Enor disappears and is replaced by an unnamed current mayor (left as a blank "<>" in the notes).[^mayor-blank]

**Recent (~100 years ago):** A wave of crime begins in Shoe → the **Church of Renewed Will** forms and begins vigilante crime patrols → a week of guerrilla warfare breaks out between the Church of Renewed Will and a crime syndicate → the city watch heavily intervenes, seemingly ending the major conflict → an explosion at the house of a Church of Renewed Will member kills several people, including (a flagged "Secret") the husband of a woman named **Romanova** and a small ice elemental → a permanent cloud forms over the western district, giving the Shady Side its name.

[^shoe-secret-unlinked]: **Structural gap.** `Shoe.md` (public) and `Shoe (Secret).md` are two entirely separate documents about the same city with no shared content and no cross-references between them — the public file never mentions Enor, the Church of Renewed Will, Romanova, or the origin of the Shady Side's permanent cloud, and the secret file never mentions any of the public file's neighborhoods, festivals, or weather system.

[^mayor-blank]: **Gap.** The current mayor of Shoe — the head of the city players are most likely to interact with — is explicitly left blank in the notes ("<>, the current mayor").

## Establishments of the Main Stretch

- **Gnim's Noodles and Nickle's Copper Coffee.** A tight, dual-identity hole-in-the-wall (noodles by day, coffee by a different name at another time) with a shared bar and open kitchen. Sells noodles (granting temporary fire resistance on a Con save) and coffee (granting darkvision and immunity to sleep on a Con save), both scaling in cost/DC by quality, quantity, and preparation style.
- **Darby's Identifying Stand.** Run by Darby, a glasses-wearing owl, who identifies items for customers — and sometimes tries to buy them off you afterward.
- **Frosty's Familiars.** Run by Frosty, an ice Weird, selling familiars of varying quality.
- **Nicks and Nack's.** Run by Nicholas (a Gorc) and Nack (a clear Goozer), selling various goods, mostly spellcasting components.
- **Shrimp's Tacos.** A small food cart run by Shrimp, a rotund, quirky-voiced elephant Wildling in a stained apron and tiny baseball cap, whose singular goal is "cook seafood tacos, he doesn't want any more, any less." Sells three tacos, each granting minor 1d6 healing plus a temporary, stacking situational bonus on a Con save: the Shrimp Taco (extended breath-holding), the Fish Taco (+5ft swim speed), and the Puffer Fish Taco (+5ft fly/levitate speed, usually unnoticed by onlookers).
- **The Wicked Wraith.** A tavern with a deliberately squashed entrance that expands upward and outward via vines and ropes as you go further in.
- **Brisk's Blessings.** A shop with backrooms where visiting priests of Pot's various faiths sell quick blessings for 1gp each: an inspirational reading (1d4 temporary HP), a "long boring prayer" (functions as a short rest), a lecture on the tenets of your faith (advantage on the next Wisdom saving throw... though the table header oddly says "Intelligence (Wisdom)"[^brisk-typo]), an admonishment of future sins (no disadvantage on ability checks for 4 hours), and one blessing whose offeror/description fields were left blank, granting a free reroll before your next long rest.
- **The Hanging Hostel.** An inn roped to the tallest tower in the Main Stretch, reachable only via a magical rope elevator.
- **Herb's.** Run by Herb, an earth Weird, selling herbal goods — including, per the notes, "a nice strong laxative tea."

[^brisk-typo]: **Minor internal inconsistency.** The Brisk's Blessings goods table lists one effect as "Advantage on Intelligence (Wisdom) saving throws" — likely a copy-paste leftover rather than an intentional dual-ability-score mechanic, but worth double-checking before using it at the table.

## Notable Manors

- **Hildenmatser Manor.** Home of the Hildenmatser family, in Platton. Three floors: the ground floor holds a breakfast room, two-story dining room, kitchen, foyer, parlor, two-story library, music room, and servants' quarters; the second floor holds bedrooms for patriarch Bryndus Hildenmatser, his daughter Zoa, and his wife Ama, plus four en-suite guest bedrooms; the third floor (a converted attic) holds a bedroom for son Tytus Hildenmatser and the guards' quarters.
- **Kane Manor / Mutt Manor.** Two separate files both describe a Platton manor tied to the same man, **Thomas Dugal Kane** ("The Mutt," founder of the Tinkerer's Guild — see Part VII) — but they don't fully agree with each other.[^kane-mutt-conflict] `Kane Manor.md` describes it as recently purchased (three years prior), staffed, at the far wall of Platton, with an eastern back garden (rod-iron gates) and western front garden (with an overlooking balcony), across four unfilled floors. `Mutt Manor.md` separately describes "the manor of Thomas Dugal Kane," also in Platton, with most rooms empty or full of packing boxes, and lists a first floor containing an entrance foyer, ballroom, study, billiard room, dining room, two separate kitchen entries, breakfast room, drawing room, and servants' quarters — plus two more floors left completely blank.

[^kane-mutt-conflict]: **Possible duplicate/conflicting entry.** `Kane Manor.md` and `Mutt Manor.md` both claim to be Thomas Dugal Kane's residence in Platton, but describe different floor layouts and don't reference each other. It's unclear whether these are meant to be the same manor described twice (with the "recently purchased, still full of boxes" detail in one echoing the other's emptiness), or two distinct properties he owns.

**Skydivers Guild.** Based in Fofam. Scavengers don heavy diving gear and miles of rope to leap from the floating city into the ocean below and explore the sunken/Shadowfell-split Abyssal City (see Part VIII).

**Assembly of Archival Ambition (AAA).** Motto: *"Knowledge above all. Academia in pursuit. Meets in a members-only room at the Library of Shoe, hidden in Platton — officially a permanently-booked meeting space, it conceals a trapdoor leading to a network of secret passages, most commonly used to reach a rooftop balcony where members are "often found drinking and boisterously discussing matters of academia."

# Part VIII — Other Named Locations

## The Plastic City

One of the nine legendary Cicebian Cities, cursed with illusion magic by Za'esitor's disciples. The city's own file skips its founding/curse story (told instead in the History file — see Part I) and focuses on its present-day rules of engagement:

- Guarded entrance, open only from dawn until two hours before dusk; a posted flag rates the day's scavenging difficulty (green, yellow, and at least one more unrecorded tier[^plastic-flag-gap]).
- **Everyone knows:** the city's internal layout changes at dawn and can't be observed from outside; dangerous inhabitants and traps guard rooms against casual exploration, worsening dramatically after dark.
- **Most know:** a magical barrier blocks teleportation in or out; any string, rope, or similar object linking two rooms together is severed at the shift; the barrier also blocks line of sight between rooms, so you can only see people sharing your current room.
- **Only a select few know:** the layout only actually changes in rooms that sunlight reaches; at dawn, doors try to slam shut to prevent this, and propping a door open (or removing it) keeps that room stable.
- **Nobody knows** (i.e., it's true but undiscovered by any NPC or PC in-fiction): the original inhabitants of the Plastic City can move between rooms freely, unaffected by the shift.

Brainstormed room concepts include a stray-cat sanctuary room, rooms permanently occupied by people who got lost and gave up trying to leave, an all-doors room, a room of attacking mirrors, an entire ~100–200 person subsistence village that survives by blacking out every room at dawn and removing all its doors, a fully underground dungeon-castle with no doors at all (where undead and scavengers have "intermingled" into their own society), a room full of unidentified potions, an academic library, and a shop ("Marico's Miraculous Unidentified Magics") selling unidentified magic items at gambling odds with immediate identification included.

[^plastic-flag-gap]: **Coverage gap.** The difficulty flag system is introduced with "green, yellow," and the sentence cuts off — presumably a red or similar top tier was intended but never written down.

## The Forest of Giant Things

Another of the nine Cicebian Cities, cursed with transmutation magic. Its "Secrets" file adds: moonlight in the forest causes a dark, corrupted form of transmutation, though anything actually created within the forest is exempt from that corruption. Home to the **Gignomes** (see Part III).

## The Abyssal City

A third Cicebian City, cursed with conjuration magic. Publicly: an ancient city deep under the ocean in the Bay of Pot, partly sustaining Fofam's economy through skilled, dangerous scavenging (the Skydivers Guild). By day, ordinary scavenging; by night, the water thins and darkens the deeper one goes, eventually breathable like a thick black fog at the lowest levels — which nobody has ever reached and returned from. The secret file elaborates: it was once a great city atop a mountain spire in the middle of the Bay of Pot; a conjuration-school disciple cast a planar shift that dropped half the city into the ocean and half into the Shadowfell simultaneously, meaning the "Abyssal" city is quite literally split across two planes at once.

## Elvilae, the Nation of Fae

Home of the first wave of Astral Elves to arrive on Pan (~10,000 years ago), deeply intertwined with Goblinoid history, and the one nation that refused to use Orcs as war-troops during the Era of Darkness (Part I). Beyond this, no further content exists.

# Part IX — Flora & Fauna

The dedicated Flora & Fauna file is brief, listing three creatures:

- **Bramblebee** — a small, fat, fuzzy green beetle with a single thorn on its head.
- **Dubfin** — a sailfish bearing two large sails.
- **Sea Kite** — a titanic, square-shaped jellyfish used by sailors to navigate the seas and ride natural currents.

Beyond these three, most of the setting's "fauna" content actually lives inside the various homebrew species files as full playable ancestries (Weirds, Wildlings, Enoki, Astralians, Goozers, Automatons — see Part III) rather than in the Flora & Fauna file itself.[^flora-fauna-thin] Additional creature-adjacent worldbuilding appears scattered in the Quick Ideas file rather than any dedicated bestiary: petrified "World Beasts" scattered across the world (a snake, a spider-crab, and a giraffe are named), "Grolks" (a catch-all term folding goblins, trolls, and orcs into one generic chaotic-evil grouping — which sits oddly next to the setting's much more detailed, non-generic treatment of Orc clans and Goblin origins elsewhere[^grolks-tension]), true automatons framed here specifically as "the janitors of the world" that eat old scrap, and animals simply named after the funny sounds they make (with "the grunter" given as the sole example).

[^flora-fauna-thin]: **Coverage gap.** For a world repeatedly described as "teeming with vibrant and strange life," the dedicated Flora & Fauna file contains only three entries.

[^grolks-tension]: **Possible tonal tension, not a hard contradiction.** The Quick Ideas file's "Grolks" concept — lumping goblins, trolls, and orcs together as generic chaotic-evil filler — sits uneasily beside the setting's much more developed treatment of Orcs (a three-clan origin myth tied directly to the god Cicebeo and the Titan Aug) and Goblins (tied into named historical events like the seven vampire lords' conflict) found elsewhere in the Lore and Peoples files. Both concepts exist in the notes; it's not clear if "Grolks" was an early, since-superseded idea or a separate lower tier of unnamed monster the more detailed peoples exist alongside.
